# ISTQB Chapter 5 Glossary

This glossary explains the key ideas in Chapter 5 in a more practical and easy-to-understand way.

## 1. Test Planning
- Test plan: A document that describes the approach, scope, resources, and schedule for testing.
- Purpose of test planning: To ensure testing is organized and aligned with project goals.
- Entry criteria: Conditions that must be satisfied before testing begins.
  - Example: Requirements must be approved before system testing starts.
- Exit criteria: Conditions that must be achieved before testing can be considered complete.
  - Example: All critical defects must be fixed and retested.
- Estimation: Predicting the amount of effort, time, and resources needed for testing.
- Test case prioritization: Deciding which tests should be executed first based on importance or risk.

## 2. Test Pyramid and Testing Quadrants
- Test pyramid: A model that suggests having many small, fast unit tests, fewer integration tests, and even fewer UI tests.
- Testing quadrants: A model that classifies testing based on both business-facing and technology-facing concerns.
- Why they matter: These ideas help teams balance cost, speed, and coverage.

## 3. Risk Management
- Risk: The possibility of a negative event or outcome.
- Project risk: A risk related to the project, such as schedule delay, resource shortage, or poor planning.
- Product risk: A risk related to the quality or behavior of the product, such as a severe defect affecting users.
- Risk likelihood: The chance that a risk will happen.
- Risk impact: The severity of the consequences if the risk occurs.
- Risk analysis: Reviewing risks to understand their likelihood and impact.
- Risk control: Actions taken to reduce or manage risks.
- Risk-based testing: Testing more thoroughly in areas with higher risk.

## 4. Test Monitoring and Test Control
- Test monitoring: Tracking progress and comparing actual results with the plan.
- Test control: Taking action to keep testing aligned with objectives.
- Example: If testing is falling behind schedule, the team may change priorities or add resources.

## 5. Test Reports and Communication
- Test progress report: A report showing what has been tested, what remains, and current status.
- Test completion report: A report created when testing is finished, summarizing results and remaining risks.
- Why reports matter: They help stakeholders make informed decisions.

## 6. Configuration Management
- Configuration management: The process of controlling changes to work products and keeping versions organized.
- Why it matters: It helps ensure the correct version of requirements, code, and test artifacts is used.

## 7. Defect Management
- Defect management: The process of reporting, tracking, and resolving defects.
- Defect report: A document that describes a defect clearly and includes relevant details such as steps to reproduce, expected result, and actual result.
- Why it matters: Good defect reports help developers understand and fix problems quickly.

## Quick memory tip
- Plan first, then monitor, control, and report.
- High-risk areas deserve more attention in testing.
